# LUX Transmitance

## Purpose

Transmitance defines the property through which condensed coherence traverses contexts while preserving meaning.

Transmitance is the fundamental property of LUX.

---

## Definition

Transmitance is the capacity of a context to allow something to pass from one state to another while preserving its essential identity.

In LUX, the transmitted element is condensed coherence.

---

## Relationship

Condensed Coherence is the substance.

Transmitance is the property.

Propagation is the emergent expression.

```text
Condensed Coherence
        ↓
Transmitance
        ↓
Propagation
```

---

## Coherence Preservation

Transmitance preserves coherence during contextual transition.

A coherent signal maintains its identity when its essential relationships remain recognizable across transformation.

```text
Identity
    ↓
Coherence
    ↓
Transmitance
    ↓
Propagation
```

---

## Context

Contexts define the conditions through which coherence is perceived.

Transmitance enables condensed coherence to move between contexts while preserving meaning.

The context reveals the degree of coherence that can traverse it.

---

## Emergent Property

Propagation emerges when transmitance is sufficient for condensed coherence to become observable in a new context.

```text
Condensed Coherence
        ↓
Transmissible Condition
        ↓
Observable Emergence
```

---

## Architectural Principle

LUX is the transmitance of condensed coherence.

Through transmitance, coherent meaning becomes capable of crossing contexts preserving its identity.
