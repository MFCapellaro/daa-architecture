# KernelLaw

## Purpose

KernelLaw defines invariant patterns derived from
relationships between concepts.

A law represents a fundamental principle that preserves
coherence across system transformations.

---

## Principle

Systems maintain identity through relationships
that remain coherent during change.

A Kernel Law does not impose external control.

It reveals a stable relationship pattern that allows
a system to adapt while preserving its essence.

---

## Structure

A KernelLaw contains:

- Identity
- Name
- Definition
- Related Relationships

The relationships define the semantic foundation
of the law.

Concepts
↓
Relationships
↓
Patterns
↓
Laws


---

## Role in the Kernel

Laws establish the constitutional principles
of a system.

They define conditions that must remain coherent
while the system transforms.

Examples:

- Identity Preservation
- Meaning Precedes Relationship
- Structure Emergence
- Grammar Emergence

---

## Dynamic Role

A law is not opposed to change.

A law defines what remains stable while change occurs.

Transformation
↓
Variability
↓
Coherent Relationships
↓
Preserved Identity


The law provides continuity across movement.

---

## Relationship with Guardians

Guardians observe whether system behavior
remains aligned with fundamental laws.

Law
↓
Guardian Observation
↓
Coherence Assessment
↓
Adaptive Response


---

## DAA Principle

A coherent system does not preserve a fixed state.

It preserves the relationships that allow
continuous adaptation.

Identity is preserved
through coherent relationships
during transformation.
