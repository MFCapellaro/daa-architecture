# Exploration Model

## Purpose

The Exploration Model defines how DAA discovers the structural characteristics of a product class from observable reality.

Exploration does not begin with a predefined schema.

It discovers the characteristics required to represent a class of products through observation, convergence, evidence and discernment.

---

## Core Principle

The structure of a product class is discovered from reality.

DAA does not impose characteristics before observing the products they must represent.

---

## Exploratory Flow

```text
Reality
    ↓
Observation
    ↓
Characteristic Candidates
    ↓
Convergence
    ↓
Factual Evidence
    ↓
Coherence
    ↓
Discernment
    ↓
Characteristic
    ↓
Product Class
    ↓
Characteristic Schema
    ↓
Product Family / Model
```

Each stage preserves the evidence of the preceding stage while enabling the next condition.

---

## Observation

Observation captures information found in existing products and their available sources.

An observation preserves its context.

Relevant context may include:

* source,
* product,
* manufacturer,
* model,
* date,
* geographic context,
* observed characteristic,
* observed value.

Observation does not establish validity.

It preserves what has been found.

---

## Characteristic Candidates

A Characteristic Candidate represents a possible structural property discovered during exploration.

Candidates remain provisional.

A candidate may originate from:

* technical documentation,
* manufacturer information,
* independent sources,
* regulatory documentation,
* repeated product observations,
* operational experience.

A candidate becomes structural only when supported by sufficient coherent evidence.

---

## Convergence

Convergence identifies characteristics that recur across independently observed products or sources.

Convergence increases evidential relevance.

```text
Observation A ──┐
Observation B ──┼──→ Convergence
Observation C ──┘
```

Repetition alone does not establish structural relevance.

Convergence must remain coherent with the product class being explored.

---

## Factual Evidence

Convergent observations become factual evidence when their basis can be independently recognized and contextualized.

Evidence preserves:

* origin,
* context,
* observed fact,
* supporting sources.

Evidence does not become knowledge merely through accumulation.

It must pass the coherence filter.

---

## Coherence

Coherence evaluates whether convergent evidence is consistent with:

* the identity of the product class,
* the meaning of the characteristic,
* the observed context,
* the available supporting evidence.

Coherence prevents isolated or incidental observations from becoming structural characteristics.

---

## Discernment

Discernment separates structurally relevant characteristics from accessory, incidental or contextual information.

Discernment determines what belongs to the characteristic structure of the class.

It does not discard information.

It determines its architectural relevance.

```text
Evidence
    ↓
Coherence
    ↓
Discernment
    ↓
Structural Relevance
```

---

## Characteristic

A Characteristic becomes part of the product class structure when its relevance has been established through coherent evidence and discernment.

A characteristic defines a reusable dimension through which products of the same class can be represented.

Examples may include:

* capacity,
* dimensions,
* weight,
* operating range,
* power,
* payload,
* compatibility.

The actual characteristics are discovered through exploration.

---

## Product Class

A Product Class represents a group of products that share a coherent set of structural characteristics.

The class is defined by its characteristic structure rather than by a manufacturer or model.

```text
Product Class
      ↓
Characteristic Schema
```

Different manufacturers may therefore provide products belonging to the same Product Class.

---

## Characteristic Schema

The Characteristic Schema represents the validated structural pattern of a Product Class.

It defines the characteristics required to represent comparable products within that class.

The schema emerges from exploration.

It is not assumed before exploration.

---

## Product Family and Model

Once a Product Class and its Characteristic Schema exist, Product Families and Models can be represented against the same structural pattern.

```text
Product Class
      ↓
Characteristic Schema
      ↓
Product Family
      ↓
Product Model
```

A Product Family may contain multiple models sharing the same product identity lineage.

Different families may coexist within the same Product Class.

---

## Comparison

Comparison is an emergent capability of the model.

When products share a validated Characteristic Schema, their characteristics can be compared without redefining the structure for each product.

```text
Characteristic Schema
        │
        ├── Product Model A
        ├── Product Model B
        └── Product Model C
```

DAA does not define comparison first.

It creates the conditions under which meaningful comparison can emerge.

---

## Evolution

Exploration remains open to refinement.

New products, sources and observations may reveal:

* missing characteristics,
* redundant characteristics,
* new relationships,
* new product classes,
* changes in structural relevance.

Therefore:

```text
Exploration
    ↓
Schema
    ↓
New Evidence
    ↓
Discernment
    ↓
Schema Refinement
```

The schema evolves while preserving the identity and coherence of the product class.

---

## Core Principle

Exploration discovers the structure.

Convergence reveals evidence.

Coherence validates relevance.

Discernment separates the structural from the incidental.

The resulting characteristics become the foundation of Product Identity.
