# Divergence Matrix

## Purpose

The Divergence Matrix identifies meaningful differences among products, families, platforms, and manufacturers within shared functional domains.

It begins from established or emerging convergence.

Where convergence asks:

> What remains common?

Divergence asks:

> How does each system remain distinct while performing related functions?

The matrix therefore provides the evidential basis for functional discernment of implementation differences.

---

## Methodological Position

Divergence follows convergence.

```text
Observation
    ↓
Normalization
    ↓
Convergence
    ↓
Divergence
    ↓
Functional Discernment
    ↓
Product Capability
    ↓
Product Class
```

A divergence cannot be meaningfully interpreted until the common functional reference has been established.

---

## Core Principle

> **Divergence reveals how shared functions are differently realized.**

A difference in terminology, specification, architecture, or platform does not automatically represent functional divergence.

The Corpus must determine what kind of difference has actually been observed.

---

# Types of Divergence

The Corpus distinguishes several preliminary forms of divergence.

## Platform Divergence

Different physical platforms perform related functions.

```text
Aerial
  ≠
Ground
```

Example:

```text
Agricultural Operation
       │
   ┌───┴───┐
   ↓       ↓
 Aerial   Ground
   │       │
P Series R Series
```

Platform divergence describes **how the system moves or operates physically**.

It does not necessarily imply functional divergence.

---

## Architectural Divergence

Different systems implement a shared function through different architectures.

```text
Same Function
      ↓
Different Architecture
```

Architecture may include:

* propulsion,
* payload arrangement,
* sensing architecture,
* control architecture,
* application mechanism,
* navigation architecture.

Architectural divergence may exist without functional divergence.

---

## Functional Divergence

Functional divergence occurs when apparently similar observations resolve into different operational roles.

Example:

```text
Obstacle Detection
      ≠
Obstacle Avoidance
```

Detection provides information.

Avoidance modifies behavior.

The two may participate in the same capability while remaining distinct functions.

---

## Characteristic Divergence

Products performing the same function may differ in measurable characteristics.

Example:

```text
Liquid Application
      ↓
Tank Capacity
      ↓
Different Values
```

A characteristic difference does not automatically imply functional divergence.

It may represent variation within the same functional class.

---

## Product Capability Divergence

Two products may share individual functions while combining them into different capabilities.

```text
Function A
+
Function B
+
Function C
   ↓
Product Capability X
```

versus:

```text
Function A
+
Function B
+
Function D
   ↓
Product Capability Y
```

Product Capability divergence may therefore emerge from differences in functional composition rather than from individual functions.

---

## Strategic Divergence

Repeated functional and architectural differences may eventually reveal different manufacturer priorities.

This level is intentionally downstream.

```text
Observed Differences
       ↓
Recurring Pattern
       ↓
Functional Interpretation
       ↓
Strategic Pattern
       ↓
Manufacturer Identity
```

The Corpus does not assign strategic identity from isolated differences.

# Functional Implementation Divergence

A shared function may be implemented differently.

For example:

```text
Liquid Application
        │
   ┌────┼────┐
   ↓    ↓    ↓
 Pump Nozzle Flow Control
```

Different combinations of these elements may produce different implementations of the same functional domain.

Therefore:

```text
Implementation Difference
        ≠
Functional Difference
```

This distinction is essential for comparison.

---

# Functional Composition

A Product capability may diverge because different systems combine functions differently.

Example:

```text
System A

Detection
   ↓
Decision
   ↓
Avoidance
```

versus:

```text
System B

Detection
   ↓
Operator Notification
   ↓
Manual Intervention
```

Both may address obstacle-related operation.

Their capabilities differ because their functional compositions differ.

Without the shared reference, a difference cannot yet be interpreted.

# Manufacturer DNA Hypothesis

Manufacturer DNA should emerge from repeated divergence within shared functional domains.

The methodological path is:

```text
Shared Function
       ↓
Different Implementation
       ↓
Repeated Pattern
       ↓
Persistent Divergence
       ↓
Manufacturer Character
```

For example, if a manufacturer repeatedly expresses a particular function through a specific architectural strategy across several product families, that recurrence may become evidence of manufacturer identity.

No such identity should be established from a single product.

---

# Divergence Confidence

The Corpus distinguishes levels of confidence.

## Level 1 — Observed Difference

Two observations are demonstrably different.

```text
A ≠ B
```

No interpretation yet.

---

## Level 2 — Structural Difference

The difference belongs to a recognizable structural domain.

```text
Aerial
≠
Ground
```

---

## Level 3 — Functional Difference

Evidence establishes that the systems perform different operational roles.

```text
Detection
≠
Avoidance
```

---

## Level 4 — Product Capability Difference

The difference produces distinct capabilities.

```text
Product Capability A
≠
Product Capability B
```

---

## Level 5 — Persistent Manufacturer Pattern

The same divergence recurs across products or families of one manufacturer.

```text
Product A
Product B
Product C
      ↓
Persistent Pattern
      ↓
Manufacturer Identity Candidate
```

This is the level at which manufacturer DNA may begin to emerge.

---

# Divergence Does Not Imply Inferiority

A divergence must never be interpreted as a quality judgment.

```text
Different
   ≠
Better
   ≠
Worse
```

The Corpus describes structural and functional differences.

Evaluation belongs to a later analytical layer.

---

# Divergence Does Not Imply Incompatibility

Two systems may diverge while remaining functionally compatible with the same operational purpose.

```text
Shared Purpose
      ↓
Shared Function
      ↓
Different Implementation
```

The divergence may represent alternative solutions rather than incompatible ones.

---

# Divergence and Product Class

Product Class remains downstream.

```text
Observed Divergence
       ↓
Functional Interpretation
       ↓
Product Capability Structure
       ↓
Recurring Pattern
       ↓
Product Class Candidate
```

A difference alone cannot define a class.

A class emerges from coherent functional structure.

---

# Divergence and Comparison

The Divergence Matrix establishes what should eventually become comparable.

It does not yet establish the comparison schema.

The sequence remains:

```text
Function
   ↓
Product Capability
   ↓
Product Class
   ↓
Characteristic Schema
   ↓
Comparison
```

Only after the Characteristic Schema emerges should technical values be compared systematically.

# Integrity

The Divergence Matrix preserves the distinction between:

```text
Difference
   ↓
Structure
   ↓
Function
   ↓
Product Capability
   ↓
Identity
```

Each interpretation must remain traceable to observations and sources.

No divergence should be elevated to a functional, capability, or identity claim without sufficient evidence.

---

## Core Principle

> **Divergence reveals how coherence is expressed differently.**

Shared purpose does not require identical implementation.

Shared function does not require identical architecture.

Different architecture does not necessarily imply different function.

The role of the Corpus is to preserve these distinctions until the evidence allows discernment.
