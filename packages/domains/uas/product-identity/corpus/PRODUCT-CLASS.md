# Product Class

## Purpose

Product Class represents a coherent semantic category through which products may be recognized as functionally comparable.

A Product Class is independent of manufacturer identity.

---

## Identity

A Product Class is defined by coherent functional meaning rather than by:

* manufacturer,
* brand,
* product family,
* model name,
* commercial terminology,
* raw specification labels.

Product Class emerges from the semantic interpretation of observable product characteristics.

---

## Precedence

Product Class follows the generative semantic sequence:

```text
Observation
    ↓
Characteristic
    ↓
Normalization
    ↓
Function
    ↓
Product Capability
    ↓
Product Class
```

Each stage preserves the semantic identity established by the preceding stage.

Product Class must therefore not be assigned directly from raw observations when their functional meaning has not yet been established.

---

## Product Family vs Product Class

Product Family and Product Class represent different semantic structures.

### Product Family

Product Family represents product organization within a manufacturer or brand context.

```text
Brand
  ↓
Family
  ↓
Product
```

It preserves manufacturer-defined product genealogy.

### Product Class

Product Class represents functional semantic identity across manufacturer boundaries.

```text
Product
   ↓
Product Capability
   ↓
Product Class
```

It establishes a common semantic reference for comparable products.

Therefore:

```text
Product Family ≠ Product Class
```

---

## Classification Scope

Product Classes primarily operate through **Cross-brand** classification.

Cross-brand classification allows products from different manufacturers to converge into a common semantic category.

```text
DJI Product ───────┐
XAG Product ────────┼──→ Product Class
TopXGun Product ────┘
```

The manufacturer and Product Family remain part of each product's identity.

Cross-brand classification does not replace them.

---

## Intra-brand Context

Product Classes may also be observed within a single manufacturer.

For example, multiple products from the same brand may express the same functional class.

This does not make the Product Class a Product Family.

The distinction remains:

```text
Family
→ manufacturer-defined organization

Class
→ semantically recognized functional category
```

---

## Class Formation

A Product Class may emerge when multiple products reveal a sufficiently coherent functional identity.

```text
Product A
   ↓
Product Capability A
   │
   ├──────────────┐
                  │
Product B         │
   ↓              │
Product Capability B ─────┼──→ Product Class
                  │
Product C         │
   ↓              │
Product Capability C ─────┘
```

The class does not require identical specifications.

It requires sufficient semantic coherence for meaningful comparison.

---

## Class Membership

A product belongs to a Product Class when its observable and interpreted capabilities satisfy the semantic conditions established for that class.

Membership must remain traceable to:

```text
Observation
    ↓
Characteristic
    ↓
Normalization
    ↓
Function
    ↓
Product Capability
    ↓
Classification
```

A product must not be classified solely because it resembles another product.

Similarity is not sufficient evidence of class membership.

---

## Cross-brand Comparability

Cross-brand Product Classes establish the semantic foundation for comparison.

For example:

```text
DJI AGRAS T50
       │
       ├── spraying capability
       │
       └──────┐
              │
XAG P150     │
       │     │
       ├── spraying capability
       │     │
       └──────┤
              ▼
      Agricultural
      Spraying UAV
```

The products may differ in:

* architecture,
* terminology,
* payload,
* dimensions,
* systems,
* manufacturer taxonomy.

The Product Class captures the coherent functional identity that permits comparison without requiring structural identity.

---

## Product Class Integrity

A Product Class must preserve:

* functional meaning,
* capability provenance,
* classification traceability,
* product identity,
* manufacturer identity,
* Product Family identity.

A Product Class must not erase the characteristics that distinguish its members.

---

## Core Principle

> Product Class establishes functional comparability across product identities without collapsing those identities into one another.

A Product Class is therefore a semantic reference, not a product definition.
