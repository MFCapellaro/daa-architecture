# Classification

## Purpose

Classification establishes coherent semantic categories through which products can be recognized and related.

Classification does not define the product.

Classification recognizes the semantic position of a product within an established classification structure.

---

## Identity

A classification preserves the distinction between:

* Product Family
* Product Class
* Classification Scope

These concepts must not be conflated.

---

## Classification Scope

Classification may operate within different semantic scopes.

### Intra-brand

Intra-brand classification operates within the context of a single manufacturer or brand.

It recognizes relationships among products that belong to the same brand context.

```text
Manufacturer
     ↓
Product Family
     ↓
Products
```

Intra-brand classification may reveal:

* product families,
* product lines,
* model relationships,
* evolutionary relationships,
* functional distinctions within a brand.

Intra-brand classification preserves the manufacturer's own product structure.

---

### Cross-brand

Cross-brand classification operates across brand boundaries.

It recognizes semantic relationships between products from different manufacturers or brands.

```text
Brand A ──┐
          │
Brand B ──┼──→ Product Class
          │
Brand C ──┘
```

Cross-brand classification does not replace the identity or family structure established by each manufacturer.

It establishes a common semantic reference through which products from different brands may be compared.

---

## Product Family

Product Family represents a coherent grouping of products within a product structure.

A family normally belongs to a manufacturer or brand context.

```text
Manufacturer
     ↓
Product Family
     ↓
Product
```

Family membership preserves product genealogy and manufacturer-defined organization.

Product Family must not be interpreted as a cross-brand Product Class.

---

## Product Class

Product Class represents a coherent semantic category recognized from observable product characteristics, functions, and capabilities.

A Product Class is independent of manufacturer identity.

```text
Product
   ↓
Observable Characteristics
   ↓
Functions
   ↓
Capabilities
   ↓
Product Class
```

Product Class enables products from different brands to be recognized as semantically comparable.

---

## Classification Relationship

Classification establishes a relationship between a product and a semantic category.

The relationship must remain traceable to the evidence from which the classification emerged.

```text
Product
   ↓
Product Capability
   ↓
Classification
   ↓
Product Class
```

Classification must not invent characteristics that are absent from the observation corpus.

---

## Precedence

Classification follows semantic discernment.

```text
Observation
    ↓
Characteristic
    ↓
Normalization
    ↓
Functional Discernment
    ↓
Product Capability
    ↓
Classification
```

Classification therefore depends on preceding semantic interpretation.

It must not precede observation, normalization, or functional discernment.

---

## Classification Integrity

A classification must preserve:

* Product identity
* Manufacturer identity
* Product Family
* Original observation traceability
* Normalized characteristic meaning
* Functional interpretation
* Product Capability identity
* Classification scope

Classification must not erase the distinctions from which the classification emerged.

---

## Comparison

Cross-brand classification enables coherent comparison.

Comparison does not begin with raw product specifications.

Comparison begins after products have been transformed into a shared semantic space.

```text
Product A ──→ Classification ──┐
                               │
Product B ──→ Classification ──┼──→ Comparison
                               │
Product C ──→ Classification ──┘
```

Products may have different:

* terminology,
* data structures,
* manufacturer taxonomies,
* specifications,
* units,
* operational contexts.

These differences do not prevent comparison when their semantic meaning can be coherently normalized and classified.

---

## Core Principle

> Classification establishes semantic comparability without erasing product identity.

Classification enables comparison.

It does not replace identity with similarity.
