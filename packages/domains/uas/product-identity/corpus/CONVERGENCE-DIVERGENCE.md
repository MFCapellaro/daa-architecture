# Convergence and Divergence

## Purpose

**Convergence and Divergence** records relationships that emerge when observations from different products, families, platforms, and manufacturers are examined together.

It is the first transversal layer of the First Corpus.

Its purpose is not to establish semantic equivalence prematurely.

It preserves the evidence from which discernment and semantic normalization may later emerge.

---

## Methodological Position

The Corpus follows this precedence:

```text
Data Origin
    ↓
Observation
    ↓
Evidence Normalization
    ↓
Convergence / Divergence
    ↓
Discernment
    ↓
Semantic Normalization
    ↓
Classification
    ↓
Product Class
    ↓
Characteristic Schema
    ↓
Comparison
```

Evidence normalization establishes a comparable representation of observations without asserting that they share the same semantic identity.

Convergence and divergence reveal recurring similarities and meaningful differences within that evidential structure.

Discernment determines which relationships are structurally meaningful.

Semantic normalization may subsequently establish a shared conceptual identity where sufficient evidence exists.

Therefore:

> Convergence does not establish equivalence.

> Divergence does not establish incompatibility.

Both are evidence for discernment.

---

## Evidence Normalization

Evidence normalization prepares observations for transversal analysis without redefining their semantic identity.

It may preserve:

* value representation,
* units,
* terminology,
* source references,
* contextual conditions,
* evidence status,
* and provenance.

```text
Observation
      ↓
Evidence Normalization
      ↓
Comparable Evidence
```

Comparable evidence does not imply semantic equivalence.

Two observations may be represented consistently while remaining conceptually distinct.

---

## Convergence

Convergence occurs when distinct observations reveal a recurring property, capability, relationship, or structural pattern.

Convergence may occur at different levels:

```text
Manufacturer
      ↓
Product Family
      ↓
Platform
      ↓
Model
      ↓
Product Capability
      ↓
Observation
```

A convergence may be:

* lexical,
* structural,
* functional,
* operational,
* technological,
* contextual.

The level of convergence must remain explicit.

---

## Lexical Convergence

Lexical convergence occurs when different products use the same or substantially similar terminology.

Example:

```text
XAG P60
Tank Capacity

XAG P100 Pro
Tank Capacity

XAG P150
Tank Capacity

XAG P150 Max
Tank Capacity
```

Repeated terminology provides evidence of a recurring characteristic.

It does not establish that the same terminology has the same meaning across manufacturers.

---

## Functional Convergence

Functional convergence occurs when different products provide a comparable capability even when terminology differs.

Example:

```text
DJI AGRAS
Spraying

XAG P Series
Spraying

XAG R Series
Spraying
```

The recurrence of spraying across aerial and ground platforms indicates a shared agricultural capability.

The physical realization of that capability may differ.

---

## Structural Convergence

Structural convergence occurs when different products organize their capabilities through recurring systems or relationships.

Within XAG, for example:

```text
P Series

Super Spray System
Super Spreader System
Terrain Radar
Obstacle Detection
```

and:

```text
R Series

Spraying System
Obstacle Detection
Terrain Adaptation
Autonomous Operation
```

The systems are not declared equivalent.

Their recurrence establishes evidence for later analysis.

---

## Cross-Platform Convergence

A Product capability may remain present while the platform changes.

Observed within XAG:

```text
Aerial Platform
      ↓
Spraying

Ground Platform
      ↓
Spraying
```

This indicates that the Product capability may belong to the agricultural purpose rather than to the physical platform itself.

The implementation remains platform-dependent.

---

## Divergence

Divergence occurs when observations that appear related reveal differences in:

* value,
* range,
* context,
* terminology,
* physical realization,
* operational constraints,
* platform,
* system architecture.

Divergence must be preserved rather than eliminated through premature semantic normalization.

---

## Model Divergence

Models belonging to the same family may share a characteristic while differing in its observed value.

Example:

```text
XAG P150
Tank Capacity → 60 L

XAG P150 Max
Tank Capacity → 70 L
```

The characteristic may converge while the value diverges.

Therefore:

```text
Characteristic convergence
        ≠
Value convergence
```

---

## Contextual Divergence

The same apparent characteristic may produce different observations according to operational context.

Example:

```text
Max Takeoff Weight
        ↓
Spraying context

Max Takeoff Weight
        ↓
Spreading context
```

A numerical value without its context is incomplete evidence.

Context must therefore remain attached to every observation.

---

## Platform Divergence

A shared Product capability may be realized through different physical platforms.

Example:

```text
Aerial
  └── Flight Speed
  └── Terrain Following

Ground
  └── Operating Speed
  └── Terrain Adaptation
  └── Climbing Ability
  └── Turning Radius
```

These observations should not be collapsed merely because both platforms operate in agricultural environments.

---

## Manufacturer Divergence

Different manufacturers may express similar capabilities through different conceptual architectures.

For example:

```text
DJI
 └── AGRAS operational systems

XAG
 ├── Super Spray System
 ├── Super Spreader System
 └── Terrain Radar
```

Manufacturer terminology is preserved before cross-brand semantic normalization.

This protects manufacturer identity from being erased by the comparison process.

---

## Evidence States

Each convergence or divergence should retain its evidential state.

```text
Observed
    ↓
Recurring
    ↓
Corroborated
    ↓
Potentially Equivalent
    ↓
Semantically Normalized
```

These states must not be conflated.

A recurring observation is not yet a normalized characteristic.

A potentially equivalent observation is not yet a confirmed equivalence.

Semantic normalization requires discernment.

---

## Discernment

Discernment examines convergence and divergence to determine what is structurally meaningful.

It separates:

```text
Relevant
   from
Accessory
```

and:

```text
Persistent
   from
Incidental
```

and:

```text
Shared Meaning
   from
Similar Appearance
```

Discernment therefore does not merely group observations.

It identifies the conditions under which observations may legitimately belong to the same conceptual structure.

---

## Discernment Preconditions

Discernment requires:

1. preserved origin,
2. contextualized observations,
3. sufficient recurrence,
4. identifiable convergence,
5. identifiable divergence,
6. traceable evidence.

Without these conditions, semantic normalization and classification remain premature.

---

## Semantic Normalization

Semantic normalization establishes a shared conceptual representation when discernment has determined that distinct observations express the same meaningful structure.

```text
Convergence / Divergence
          ↓
      Discernment
          ↓
Semantic Normalization
          ↓
Shared Conceptual Identity
```

Semantic normalization therefore differs from evidence normalization.

Evidence normalization makes observations comparable.

Semantic normalization establishes justified conceptual identity.

```text
Evidence Normalization
        ↓
Comparable Evidence

Semantic Normalization
        ↓
Comparable Meaning
```

Neither process erases the origin of the evidence.

## What Is Not Yet Established

The current Corpus does not yet establish:

* definitive Product Classes,
* a final Characteristic Schema,
* cross-manufacturer semantic equivalence,
* manufacturer DNA,
* platform taxonomy,
* capability ontology.

These remain subjects for further observation and discernment.

---

## Relationship to Comparison

Comparison is downstream from discernment and semantic normalization.

The sequence is:

```text
Observation
      ↓
Evidence Normalization
      ↓
Convergence / Divergence
      ↓
Discernment
      ↓
Semantic Normalization
      ↓
Characteristic Schema
      ↓
Comparison
```

Comparison should therefore not be constructed directly from raw product specifications.

It should emerge from a previously discerned semantic structure.

---

## Core Principle

> **Convergence reveals recurrence.**

> **Divergence reveals distinction.**

> **Discernment determines significance.**

> **Semantic normalization establishes justified shared meaning.**

Together they allow the Corpus to transform heterogeneous observations into coherent knowledge without erasing the identity of their origins.

---

## Integrity

Convergence and divergence records must remain traceable to their originating observations.

No relationship should be promoted to equivalence, classification, or normalized meaning without sufficient evidence.

The Corpus preserves uncertainty until discernment can legitimately resolve it.
