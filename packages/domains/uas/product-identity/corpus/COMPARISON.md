# Comparison

## Purpose

Comparison establishes meaningful relationships between members of the same Product Class through normalized characteristics.

Comparison does not merely place values side by side.

It reveals convergence, divergence, proportion, relation, and structural difference within a shared semantic context.

---

## Methodological Position

Comparison follows the complete preparation of the comparative structure.

```text
Source
  ↓
Observation
  ↓
Normalization
  ↓
Function
  ↓
Product Capability
  ↓
Classification
  ↓
Product Class
  ↓
Characteristic Schema
  ↓
Characteristic Normalization
  ↓
Comparison
  ↓
Convergence / Divergence
  ↓
Comparative Discernment
  ↓
Knowledge
  ↓
Evolution
```

Comparison therefore belongs to the comparative regime of the Corpus.

Convergence and divergence are not prerequisites for comparison.

They are relationships revealed through comparison.

Comparative discernment interprets those relationships.

---

# Core Principle

> **Comparison makes normalized differences and similarities observable within a shared semantic structure.**

Comparison requires common ground.

Without common ground, difference cannot be interpreted coherently.

---

# Comparison Requires Context

A comparison is meaningful only when its participants share a defined analytical context.

```text
Product Class
      ↓
Shared Context
      ↓
Characteristic Schema
      ↓
Comparison
```

Comparison must therefore establish:

* what is being compared,
* why it is being compared,
* under which class,
* through which characteristics,
* and under which conditions.

---

# Comparison Is Not Ranking

Comparison does not automatically determine a winner.

```text
Comparison
    ≠
Ranking
```

Comparison reveals relationships.

Ranking introduces an evaluative criterion.

```text
Comparison
   ↓
Difference
   ↓
Interpretation
   ↓
Evaluation
```

Evaluation may be appropriate for a specific purpose, but it must not be confused with comparison itself.

---

# Comparison Is Not Selection

Comparison may support selection.

It does not perform selection automatically.

```text
Comparison
    ↓
Evidence
    ↓
Decision
```

The decision depends on purpose and criteria beyond the existence of differences.

---

# Comparison Is Not Marketing

Manufacturer claims remain source evidence.

Comparison establishes relationships among normalized observations.

```text
Manufacturer Claim
      ↓
Observation
      ↓
Normalization
      ↓
Comparison
```

The comparison must not reproduce marketing hierarchy as analytical hierarchy.

---

# Common Ground

Comparison requires a shared reference.

```text
Product A ─┐
Product B ─┼──→ Product Class
Product C ─┘
```

and:

```text
Product Class
      ↓
Characteristic Schema
      ↓
Comparable Representation
```

The shared structure makes differences meaningful.

---

# Pairwise Comparison

Two products may be compared directly.

```text
Product A
    ↕
Product B
```

The comparison operates over the characteristics defined by the schema.

```text
Characteristic 1
Characteristic 2
Characteristic 3
       ↓
A ↔ B
```

---

# Multi-Product Comparison

More than two products may be compared simultaneously.

```text
             Product Class
                  │
       ┌──────────┼──────────┐
       ↓          ↓          ↓
      DJI        XAG      TopXGun
```

The comparison should preserve the same characteristic semantics across all participants.

---

# Characteristic-Level Comparison

Comparison may occur at the level of individual characteristics.

```text
Characteristic
      ↓
Product A
Product B
Product C
```

This reveals local convergence and divergence.

---

# Structural Comparison

Comparison may also occur at the level of Product capability structure.

```text
Product Capability Structure A
          ↕
Product Capability Structure B
```

This is more significant than isolated numerical differences.

Two products may have different values while sharing the same structural capability.

---

# Functional Comparison

Functions may be compared when their normalized meanings are established.

```text
Function A
   ↕
Function B
```

The comparison should determine whether they are:

* equivalent,
* related,
* complementary,
* overlapping,
* or functionally distinct.

---

# Product Capability Comparison

Capabilities can be compared according to their structure and operational expression.

```text
Product Capability A
     ↕
Product Capability B
```

The relevant question is not merely whether both products possess a feature.

It is:

> **Do they express a comparable Product capability?**

---

# Convergence

Comparison may reveal convergence.

```text
Product A ──┐
            ├──→ Shared Characteristic
Product B ──┘
```

Convergence indicates that different products express a common semantic, functional, or structural condition.

Convergence is therefore an observed relationship produced by comparison.

---

# Divergence

Comparison may reveal divergence.

```text
Product A
     │
     │
     ↓
Structural Difference
     ↑
     │
Product B
```

Divergence becomes meaningful when it can be connected to a characteristic, function, capability, or implementation structure.

Divergence is therefore an observed relationship produced by comparison.

---

# Convergence and Divergence

They should be considered together.

```text
              Comparison
                  │
          ┌───────┴───────┐
          ↓               ↓
     Convergence      Divergence
          │               │
          └───────┬───────┘
                  ↓
       Comparative Discernment
```

Convergence establishes common ground.

Divergence establishes distinction.

Comparative discernment interprets their relationship.

---

# Comparison Does Not Erase Difference

Normalization establishes comparability.

Comparison makes difference visible.

```text
Normalization
      ↓
Common Representation
      ↓
Comparison
      ↓
Meaningful Difference
```

The purpose of normalization is therefore not to eliminate divergence.

It is to make divergence interpretable.

---

# Quantitative Comparison

Numerical characteristics may be compared directly when they share:

* semantic identity,
* unit,
* scale,
* and relevant conditions.

```text
Product A: 40 L
Product B: 50 L
```

The difference can then be represented as:

```text
Absolute Difference
Relative Difference
Ratio
```

The appropriate representation depends on analytical purpose.

---

# Qualitative Comparison

Categorical characteristics require semantic comparison.

```text
Product A: Assisted
Product B: Autonomous
```

The comparison should not treat these as numerical values.

It should preserve the semantic relationship between categories.

---

# Structural Comparison

Structural characteristics compare architecture or composition.

```text
Structure A
     ↕
Structure B
```

This may reveal differences that numerical specifications do not capture.

---

# Contextual Comparison

A value should be compared only when its conditions are sufficiently compatible.

```text
Value A + Condition A
          ↕
Value B + Condition B
```

If conditions differ materially, the comparison must preserve that difference.

---

# Conditional Comparability

Two values may be numerically comparable but operationally incomparable.

For example:

```text
Flight Time A
   ↕
Flight Time B
```

may require:

```text
Payload
Wind
Battery
Operating Mode
```

before the comparison becomes meaningful.

---

# Missing Data

Missing values must not be silently interpreted.

```text
Product A: 40 L
Product B: Unknown
```

This is not:

```text
40 L > 0 L
```

Unknown remains unknown.

---

# Unresolved Data

Conflicting evidence must remain visible.

```text
Product A
40 L / 45 L
    ↓
Unresolved
```

Comparison should not conceal the conflict.

---

# Not Applicable

A characteristic that does not apply should remain distinct from missing data.

```text
Product A: 40 L
Product B: Not Applicable
```

This is not a numerical comparison.

The distinction itself may be analytically meaningful.

---

# Comparison Conditions

Every comparison should be interpretable through:

```text
Comparison
├── Product Class
├── Purpose
├── Characteristics
├── Normalization Rules
├── Conditions
├── Sources
├── Evidence Status
└── Comparison Result
```

This makes the comparison auditable.

---

# Comparison and Manufacturer

Manufacturer remains an independent dimension.

```text
Product Class
      ↓
Comparison
      ├── DJI
      ├── XAG
      └── TopXGun
```

The comparison should not favor or privilege a manufacturer.

---

# Comparison and Manufacturer DNA

Comparison provides the reference space in which manufacturer-specific patterns can emerge.

```text
Shared Product Class
        ↓
Common Characteristics
        ↓
Comparison
        ↓
Repeated Divergences
        ↓
Persistent Pattern
        ↓
Manufacturer Signature
```

The signature is not assumed.

It emerges from repeated differences across comparable products.

---

# Comparison Across Product Families

A Product Class may intersect several manufacturer families.

```text
             Product Class
          ┌──────┼──────┐
          ↓      ↓      ↓
       Family A Family B Family C
          │      │      │
       Product Product Product
```

This allows comparison to transcend manufacturer taxonomy.

---

# Comparison and Product Family

Product Family remains useful context.

It may reveal:

* technological lineage,
* product evolution,
* manufacturer strategy,
* implementation continuity.

But Family membership does not replace Product Class membership.

Comparison should therefore preserve both dimensions.

# Comparison Relationships

Comparison may establish several relationship types.

### Equivalent

Two characteristics express effectively the same semantic condition.

### Similar

Two characteristics share substantial meaning but differ in relevant aspects.

### Different

The characteristics represent distinct conditions.

### Complementary

Two capabilities differ but contribute to a shared operational purpose.

### Superior / Inferior

Only valid when an explicit evaluation criterion exists.

### Incomparable

The semantic or contextual conditions are insufficient for valid comparison.

---

# Incomparability

Incomparability is a valid result.

```text
Product A
    ×
Product B
```

may occur when:

* characteristics have different meanings,
* conditions are incompatible,
* evidence is insufficient,
* products do not belong to the same analytical class,
* or normalization cannot establish a valid common representation.

The Corpus must not force comparability where none exists.

---

# Comparison Matrix

A comparison may be represented as:

```text
                 Characteristic
                      │
          ┌───────────┼───────────┐
          ↓           ↓           ↓
        Product A   Product B   Product C
```

The matrix is a representation.

It is not the comparison itself.

Meaning remains dependent on the definitions, normalization, and context established upstream.

---

# Comparison Result

A comparison should produce more than a list of differences.

Conceptually:

```text
Comparison
    ↓
Observed Relations
    ├── Convergence
    ├── Divergence
    ├── Similarity
    ├── Difference
    └── Incomparability
```

These relations become evidence for further discernment.

# Comparison and Discernment

Comparison produces a new evidence surface.

```text
Comparison
      ↓
Convergence / Divergence
      ↓
Comparative Discernment
      ↓
New Meaning
```

The original discernment established the capabilities and structures that made comparison possible.

Comparative discernment interprets patterns revealed by comparison.

This creates a second-order discernment process.

---

# Comparison and Evolution

Comparison may reveal changes across versions, families, or generations.

```text
Product A
    ↓
Product B
    ↓
Product C
```

Repeated differences may reveal:

* adaptation,
* capability expansion,
* capability reduction,
* architectural change,
* or strategic direction.

Comparison therefore becomes an observational mechanism for evolution.

---

# Comparison and Knowledge

Comparison can generate new knowledge when repeated relationships become discernible.

```text
Comparison
    ↓

    ↓

    ↓

```

Knowledge generated from comparison may subsequently feed:

```text
Knowledge
```

The comparative layer therefore participates in the broader Knowledge Flow.

---

# Comparison Feedback

Comparison may reveal problems upstream.

```text
Comparison
   ↓
Unexpected Result
   ↓
Schema Review
   ↓
Normalization Review
   ↓
Classification Review
   ↓
New Comparison
```

This is not failure.

It is evidence that the comparative structure has revealed a previously hidden distinction.

---

# Comparison Integrity

Comparison must preserve:

### Common Ground

Products are compared within a defined semantic context.

### Provenance

Every value remains traceable to its source.

### Normalization

Values share a justified representation.

### Context

Conditions remain visible.

### Uncertainty

Unknown and unresolved values remain explicit.

### Difference

Meaningful divergence is preserved.

### Purpose

Comparison remains connected to its analytical objective.

### Non-Prejudgment

The result is not determined before the comparison is performed.

---

# Comparison States

## Prepared

Product Class and Characteristic Schema are established.

## Comparable

Sufficient normalization exists to compare the selected characteristics.

## Partial

Some characteristics remain unknown or unresolved.

## Conditional

Comparison is valid only under specified conditions.

## Incomplete

The schema or evidence is insufficient.

## Incomparable

A valid semantic comparison cannot be established.

These states should remain explicit.
