# Remote Pilot Training

## Purpose

Represents the relationship between Civil Aviation Instruction Centers
(CIAC) and the training pathway for remote pilots.

## Regulatory Context

ANAC establishes that remote pilot training is conducted through
authorized Civil Aviation Instruction Centers.

The training must follow an instructional program recognized by the
Aeronautical Authority.

## Training Pathway

```text
Remote Pilot Candidate
        ↓
Authorized CIAC
        ↓
Recognized Training Program
        ↓
Theoretical and Practical Instruction
        ↓
Theoretical-Practical Examination
        ↓
ANAC / Designated Examiner
        ↓
Remote Pilot License
```

## CIAC Role

A CIAC is an ecosystem participant that provides instruction.

It does not issue the remote pilot license merely by providing training.

The licensing process includes the examination and authorization process
established by ANAC.

## Current RPA/RPAS Framework

ANAC states that all CIACs certified under RAAC Part 141 may manage
authorization to provide remote pilot and RPAS instructor courses for the
applicable categories.

The 2025 RPA/RPAS regulatory framework therefore opened the possibility
for multiple CIACs to participate in remote pilot training rather than
concentrating the activity in a single training organization.

## Regulatory Transition

Resolution ANAC 293/2026 approved a new edition of RAAC Part 141.

CIACs must adapt their procedures, manuals and instructional programs
according to the transitional regime established by the resolution.

From January 1, 2027, new courses must comply with the new Part 141
requirements.

## Ecosystem Relationship

```text
ANAC
  │
  ├── regulates
  │
  ▼
CIAC
  │
  ├── provides
  │
  ▼
Training
  │
  ├── develops
  │
  ▼
Pilot Competence
  │
  └── contributes to

```

## Separation of Concerns

Training is not a characteristic of:

* the pilot;
* the aircraft;
* the product.

Training is a capability provided by an ecosystem organization under a
regulatory framework.
